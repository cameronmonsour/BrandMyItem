This is a real marketplace-trust problem — you're describing something close to what Kickstarter, Upwork, and influencer platforms like Aspire/Grin have all had to solve. Let me lay out how this typically gets structured, since there are a few interlocking pieces: escrow timing, identity verification, and staged proof-of-performance.

## The core mechanic: don't pay the owner all at once

The brand pays 100% upfront (item price + 20% fee), but that money shouldn't go straight to the item owner. It should sit in **escrow** and release in pieces tied to proof:

- **On purchase**: brand's card is charged in full. Funds held by the platform (via Stripe Connect or similar), not disbursed.
- **Milestone 1 (~20-30% release)**: owner uploads proof the logo/sticker is actually applied.
- **Ongoing (~monthly or per check-in)**: remaining balance releases in installments tied to the check-in cadence.
- **Final release**: last chunk pays out at the 12-month mark, or gets prorated if the owner drops out early.

## Identity verification for the owner (not the brand)

- **KYC before first payout**: government ID + selfie match (Stripe Identity, Persona, or Onfido) required before any funds release, not before posting.
- **Bank account or verified payout method**, not just a card.
- Optionally: linked phone number + address for higher-value items.

## What happens when someone doesn't follow through

- **No proof photo within X days of claim** → auto-refund to the brand, no dispute needed.
- **Proof given, then goes dark on check-ins** → prorate; remainder refunds automatically.
- **Photo proof is fake/reused** → spot-check + brand can flag, human review, strike system tied to verified identity.

## The part that actually needs a lawyer, not me

Holding other people's money and releasing it on a schedule is regulated — in the US this brushes up against money transmitter rules. The practical way almost everyone handles this is Stripe Connect's escrow/delayed-payout features rather than holding funds yourself. I'd treat that as a hard requirement, not a nice-to-have, and loop in someone who actually knows payments compliance before this handles real money.
