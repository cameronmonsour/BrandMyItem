## 1. Shipping Time Representation & Delay Notice (Mail Order Rule compliance)

This is the part with an actual regulation behind it — 16 CFR Part 435. Two pieces: what you promise up front, and what happens if you can't keep it.

### 1a. The listing-page representation

> *Once this item is fully funded, IRLi LLC will purchase the item and arrange for branding to be applied. If you choose to apply the branding yourself, expect delivery within 30 days of full funding. If IRLi LLC applies the branding for you, expect delivery within 60 days of full funding. If we cannot meet this window, we will notify you before it passes and give you the choice to wait or receive a full refund.*

⚠️ **The "reasonable basis" requirement attaches here.** The rule doesn't just require you to say a number — it requires you to actually believe, based on real information, that you can hit it. If your materials/manufacturing partner has ever taken longer than 60 days, that history needs to inform this number, not just a target you're hoping for.

### 1b. Delay notice (send this before the promised date passes, if you're going to miss it)

> **Subject: Your BrandMyItem order — a quick update on timing**
>
> Hi [Brand Contact Name],
>
> Your campaign for [Item Name] completed funding on [Date], and we said the item would ship by [Original Promised Date]. We're running behind — here's where things stand: [one sentence, real reason].
>
> You have two options:
> 1. **Wait.** We now expect to ship by [New Date]. No action needed if this works for you.
> 2. **Cancel and get a full refund.** Reply to this email or click [link] and we'll refund your full payment within [X] business days.
>
> If we don't hear from you by [Date — must be before or at the new ship date], we'll take that as your consent to the new timeline, **as long as the new delay is 30 days or less past the original date.** If the delay is longer than that, we need to hear from you directly, or we'll issue your refund automatically.

⚠️ **That last sentence is doing real regulatory work** — the FTC rule specifically distinguishes "delay ≤30 days past original promise" (silence can count as consent) from "delay >30 days" (requires actual affirmative response or automatic refund). Don't let this get simplified away in editing; it's not boilerplate, it's the actual rule.

## 2. Refund Policy

> **Full refund, no exceptions:** If IRLi LLC cannot deliver the item at all — the retailer discontinues it, it becomes unavailable, or we otherwise cannot fulfill the order — you receive a full refund automatically, without needing to request it.
>
> **No refund for subjective dissatisfaction:** Once the item and branding placement have been shown to you and approved at the time of purchase, IRLi LLC does not offer refunds because you later decide you don't like how it looks, changed your mind, or found a similar opportunity elsewhere.
>
> **Refund for verified defects:** If the item arrives materially different from what was shown at purchase, or the branding materials are defective due to a manufacturing or application error by IRLi LLC or its fulfillment partner, you are entitled to a refund or replacement at IRLi LLC's discretion. This does not cover normal wear, owner misuse, or damage occurring after delivery.

⚠️ Note from earlier in our conversation, worth restating here: this policy governs *disputes*, not *chargebacks*. If a brand disputes the charge with their card issuer directly, Visa/Mastercard rules decide the outcome independent of what this document says.

>
> This license does not transfer ownership of the Marks, does not permit use beyond the specific funded item and campaign, and terminates if the campaign is cancelled or refunded (except as needed to wind down existing materials already produced).

> IRLi LLC is not responsible for verifying that a brand's uploaded Marks do not infringe a third party's trademark, and reserves the right to remove any listing using Marks that are reported as infringing.

⚠️ That last sentence matters — without it, you could be read as implicitly vouching for every logo anyone uploads.

## 4. Owner Obligations & FTC Disclosure

> By accepting a funded item, the owner agrees to: (a) apply and maintain the sponsor branding as shown on the listing for the full 12-month term; (b) submit periodic photo check-ins as described on the listing; and (c) if the owner posts about the item on social media, clearly and conspicuously disclose the sponsorship (for example, "#ad" or "Sponsored by [Brand]") in accordance with FTC guidance, regardless of whether IRLi LLC requires the post.

This is the clause that operationalizes the FTC Endorsement Guides finding from our research — the disclosure obligation exists the moment the owner posts, not only if you require them to.

> **No warranty.** The item is provided "as is." IRLi LLC disclaims all warranties beyond those provided by the item's original manufacturer.
>
> **Limitation of liability.** IRLi LLC's total liability to any brand or owner arising from a campaign is limited to the amount actually paid for that campaign. IRLi LLC is not liable for indirect, incidental, or consequential damages, including lost business or lost profits.
>
> **Force majeure.** IRLi LLC is not liable for delays or failures caused by events outside its reasonable control, including manufacturer shortages, shipping carrier delays, or acts of God — though the shipping-delay notice and refund process above still applies.

## 6. Governing Law & Dispute Resolution

> These Terms are governed by the laws of the State of California, without regard to conflict-of-law principles.
>
> Any dispute arising from these Terms will first be attempted to be resolved informally by contacting [email]. If not resolved within 30 days, either party may pursue binding arbitration in [County], California, on an individual basis and not as a class, collective, or representative action, **except that nothing in this section waives either party's right to seek public injunctive relief in any forum, as required under California law (Civ. Code § 3513; McGill v. Citibank, N.A. (2017) 2 Cal.5th 945).** Either party may also bring an individual claim in small claims court where eligible.

⚠️ **This is now McGill-compliant in structure** — it no longer attempts a blanket "any forum" waiver, which is exactly what made the Citibank clause in *McGill* void. This is a meaningfully better draft than before, but "structurally follows the right pattern" and "an attorney has confirmed this exact language works for your exact business" are still two different things. This is the one clause in this whole document I'd most want an attorney's eyes on before it goes live, specifically because getting it wrong doesn't just fail — it can render the *entire* arbitration section unenforceable, sending every dispute to open court instead.

>
> **Description of Service.** BrandMyItem is a marketplace where individuals ("Owners") post everyday items they want, brands ("Sponsors") purchase advertising placement on those items, and IRLi LLC purchases the item once a listing is fully funded, arranges for sponsor branding to be applied, and ships it to the Owner.
>
> **Payment timing.** Sponsors are charged the listed spot price plus a 20% platform fee immediately upon purchase. There is no waiting period, hold, or campaign-completion trigger before a charge occurs.
>
> **Unsold listings.** If a listing does not sell out within 60 days of being posted, all Sponsors who purchased spots on that listing will be automatically refunded in full, and the listing will be removed or reset.

⚠️ This is the clause that implements the fix you locked in for the "no deadline on an unsold listing" gap. **60 days is now finalized** — chosen over 30 (too short for multi-spot/expensive listings to realistically sell out) and over 90 (doubles brand-money exposure time for a marginal gain in completions).
